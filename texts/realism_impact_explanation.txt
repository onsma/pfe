Why performance became more realistic after realism modifications
=================================================================

Observed metrics (Option A, realism v2):
- RandomForest: accuracy=0.765, precision=0.829, recall=0.782, f1=0.805, auc=0.820
- XGBoost:      accuracy=0.745, precision=0.783, recall=0.815, f1=0.798, auc=0.802

These results are better for a realistic project than the previous near-perfect scores.


1) What happened before (why scores were too high)
--------------------------------------------------
In the first synthetic version:
- the target was generated with a latent formula strongly tied to a few dominant variables
  (especially score_bfpme_global and dpd-like behavior variables),
- there was limited randomness,
- train/test split was random, so train and test had very similar distributions.

Result:
- models could recover the target logic almost directly,
- metrics were artificially high (AUC close to 0.99), which is uncommon in real credit risk data.


2) What changed in realism v2
-----------------------------

2.1 Added stochasticity/noise
- A stronger random component was added to latent target generation.
- This increases irreducible uncertainty (real life always has unknown factors).

Effect:
- the same input profile no longer maps to almost deterministic output,
- model discrimination decreases to more realistic levels.


2.2 Reduced dominance of top predictors
- We lowered the relative weight of dominant variables (score_bfpme_global, dpd),
  and spread risk signal over more features (macro, leverage, dscr, stress, protections).

Effect:
- model cannot rely on one or two “shortcut” features,
- learning becomes harder but more representative of operational reality.


2.3 Injected realistic contradictions
- We introduced counter-intuitive cases:
  - some good dossiers default,
  - some risky dossiers survive.

Effect:
- this breaks deterministic patterns,
- forces models to handle overlap between classes (common in real portfolios),
- reduces overconfident separation.


2.4 Moved from random split to temporal split
- Train = older dossiers, Test = newer dossiers.
- This introduces natural distribution shift (time drift).

Effect:
- evaluation is stricter and closer to production use,
- performance drops compared to random split, but credibility increases.


3) Why the new metrics are “better” for credibility
---------------------------------------------------
- AUC around 0.80-0.82 is plausible for many real risk settings.
- Precision/recall trade-offs look realistic:
  - RandomForest has slightly better precision,
  - XGBoost has slightly better recall.
- The gap between models is now modest, which is common in practical deployments.

So, lower metrics here do not mean the project got worse.
They mean the evaluation setting became more realistic and more trustworthy.


4) Practical interpretation for BFPME/client presentation
---------------------------------------------------------
Main message:
- v1 validated technical pipeline consistency,
- v2 validated robustness under more realistic assumptions.

What to highlight:
- model performance remains solid after realism stress,
- the methodology is now more defensible for next phases,
- temporal validation and contradiction handling improve credibility before real-data migration.
